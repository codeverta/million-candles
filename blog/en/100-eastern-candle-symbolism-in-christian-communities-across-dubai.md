## **In-Depth Discussion**

### **1. The Background and Origin of Eastern Candle Traditions**

Eastern candles have their roots in the centuries-old traditions of Eastern Orthodox and Eastern Catholic churches. These practices originate from countries such as Greece, Russia, Armenia, Ukraine, Lebanon, Egypt, and Ethiopia. As expatriates from these regions moved to Dubai, they brought their cultural and religious heritage with them.

Today, you can find vibrant Eastern Christian communities in Jebel Ali, Oud Metha, Jumeirah, Deira, and Sharjah (which many Dubai residents attend), where candle rituals remain a central part of spiritual life.

#### **1.1 Light as a Core Symbol in Eastern Christianity**

The symbolism of light is one of the most powerful elements in Eastern Christian theology. In this tradition, candles represent:

* **Christ as the Eternal Light of the World**
* **Divine truth illuminating the human soul**
* **Purity and spiritual cleansing**
* **Victory over sin and death**
* **The presence of God during worship**

This symbolism resonates deeply among Christian communities in Dubai, many of whom view candles as a bridge connecting them to their homeland traditions.

### **3. Important Eastern Candle Symbols Explained**

Dubai’s Christian communities from Greece, Lebanon, India, Russia, and Africa continue to uphold several important candle symbols.

#### **3.1 Trikirion (Three-Branched Candle)**

A candle with three branches used by bishops.
Symbolizes:

* The **Holy Trinity**
* God’s unity and divine mystery

Seen especially in Greek Orthodox and Russian Orthodox churches in Dubai.

#### **3.2 Dikirion (Two-Branched Candle)**

Represents:

* The dual nature of Christ (fully divine, fully human)

Used during blessings and major liturgical moments.

#### **3.3 Paschal Candle**

The large candle used during Easter commemorations, symbolizing:

* Christ rising from the dead
* The triumph of life over darkness
* Eternal divine presence

In Dubai’s Roman Catholic and some Eastern Catholic congregations, you will also see Paschal candles decorated with:

* Alpha and Omega
* A cross
* Incense nails

#### **3.4 Beeswax Candles**

Beeswax candles hold special significance in Eastern traditions because:

* They symbolize the purity of Christ
* They burn cleanly and naturally
* The scent represents spiritual sweetness

In Dubai, many churches import beeswax candles from Greece, Lebanon, and Armenia.

### **4. Eastern Candle Rituals Practiced in Dubai**

#### **4.1 Easter (Pascha) Midnight Candle Ceremony**

One of the most meaningful religious events in Dubai is the Pascha midnight service. Churches dim their lights, creating deep silence. Then, the priest lights a single candle, symbolizing Christ’s resurrection. Members of the congregation pass the flame to each other, filling the church with light.

This ceremony symbolizes:

* **Renewal**
* **Hope**
* **Victory over darkness**
* **Unity among believers**

Parishes such as St. Thomas Orthodox Cathedral in Oud Metha and St. Mary’s Catholic Church witness large candlelit gatherings each year.

#### **4.2 Candle Lighting Upon Entering Church**

In many Orthodox churches in Dubai, believers light a candle as soon as they enter. The ritual symbolizes:

* A prayer offering
* A connection to God
* Gratitude and remembrance

This simple act is deeply emotional for expatriates far from home.

#### **4.3 Baptism Candles**

During baptisms, families bring decorated candles that represent:

* A child receiving divine light
* A spiritual beginning

In Dubai, baptism candles are often adorned with lace, icons, flowers, or personalized decorations.

#### **4.4 Memorial Candles**

Memorial candles are used to:

* Pray for departed loved ones
* Honor ancestors
* Seek comfort and peace

Many Christian families in Dubai maintain small home altars where they light candles for these occasions.
