## Delving into the Longevity of Aromatherapy Candles

The duration a candle burns is influenced by several factors, from the type of wax used to the size of the wick and even the ambient temperature. Understanding these variables is crucial in selecting a long-lasting aromatherapy candle that suits your needs.

### 1. Wax Types: The Foundation of Candle Longevity

The type of wax significantly impacts a candle's burn time and scent throw. Here's a breakdown of the most common wax types used in aromatherapy candles:

- **Soy Wax:** Soy wax is a popular choice for its eco-friendliness and clean burn. It's derived from soybeans, making it a renewable resource. Soy wax candles typically burn slower than paraffin wax candles, offering a longer burn time and a more subtle scent throw. They are also less likely to produce soot, making them a healthier option for indoor air quality. Soy wax is a great option to consider.

- **Beeswax:** Beeswax is a natural wax produced by honeybees. It's known for its clean, bright burn and its ability to purify the air. Beeswax candles have a naturally sweet, honey-like scent and can burn for a considerable amount of time. While they are often more expensive than soy wax candles, their longevity and air-purifying qualities make them a worthwhile investment.

- **Coconut Wax:** Coconut wax is a relatively new player in the candle market, but it's quickly gaining popularity due to its exceptional burn time and scent throw. Made from the meat of coconuts, this wax is sustainable and clean-burning. Coconut wax candles tend to hold fragrance well and burn evenly, minimizing wax wastage.

- **Paraffin Wax:** Paraffin wax is a petroleum-based wax that is commonly used in candle making due to its affordability. However, it's not the most environmentally friendly option and can release toxins into the air when burned. Paraffin wax candles typically have a shorter burn time compared to soy, beeswax, or coconut wax candles.

**Recommendation:** For the longest burn time and cleanest burn, opt for candles made from beeswax or coconut wax. Soy wax is a good alternative if you're looking for a more budget-friendly option.

### 2. Wick Matters: The Highway for Scent

The wick plays a vital role in how a candle burns and how effectively it releases its fragrance. The material and size of the wick directly impact burn time, scent throw, and overall candle performance.

- **Wick Material:** Wicks are typically made from cotton, paper, or wood. Cotton wicks are the most common and come in various sizes and shapes. Wooden wicks, on the other hand, create a crackling sound when burned, adding to the ambiance of the candle. The material of the wick affects how it draws the wax and how evenly the candle burns.

- **Wick Size:** Choosing the right wick size is crucial for optimal candle performance. A wick that is too small won't generate enough heat to melt the wax properly, resulting in tunneling (when the wax only melts in the center of the candle). A wick that is too large can cause the candle to burn too quickly and produce excessive smoke and soot.

- **Wick Trimming:** Regularly trimming the wick to about ¼ inch before each use is essential for maintaining a clean and even burn. Trimming the wick prevents the candle from smoking and helps to prolong its burn time.

**Recommendation:** Look for candles with properly sized wicks made from high-quality cotton or wood. Always trim the wick before each use to ensure a clean and long-lasting burn.

### 3. Scent Load and Fragrance Oils

The amount of fragrance oil used in a candle and the quality of the fragrance oil can also affect its longevity.

- **Scent Load:** The scent load refers to the percentage of fragrance oil used in relation to the wax. A higher scent load doesn't necessarily mean a stronger scent or a longer burn time. In fact, too much fragrance oil can interfere with the wax's ability to burn properly, leading to uneven burning and a shorter lifespan.

- **Fragrance Oil Quality:** The quality of the fragrance oil is also important. High-quality fragrance oils tend to be more concentrated and require less to achieve the desired scent throw. They also tend to be more stable and less likely to degrade over time, resulting in a longer-lasting scent.

**Recommendation:** Choose candles with a balanced scent load and made with high-quality fragrance oils. Natural essential oils are often preferred for aromatherapy candles due to their therapeutic properties and clean scent.

### 4. Jar Shape and Size

The shape and size of the candle jar can also influence its burn time and overall performance.

- **Jar Shape:** Candles in wider jars tend to burn more evenly than candles in narrow jars. This is because the heat from the flame is distributed more evenly across the surface of the wax, preventing tunneling.

- **Jar Size:** Larger candles naturally have a longer burn time than smaller candles. However, it's important to consider the overall size of the room when choosing a candle size. A small candle may not be effective in a large room, while a large candle may be overpowering in a small room.

**Recommendation:** Opt for candles in wider jars and choose a size that is appropriate for the size of the room you intend to use it in.

## Burning Practices for Optimal Candle Longevity

In addition to choosing the right candle, following proper burning practices can significantly extend its lifespan.

- **First Burn is Crucial:** Allow the candle to burn long enough for the entire surface of the wax to melt completely during the first burn. This prevents tunneling and ensures an even burn throughout the candle's lifespan.

- **Burn Time:** Avoid burning candles for more than 4 hours at a time. Overburning can cause the candle to overheat and shorten its lifespan.

- **Placement:** Place candles on a heat-resistant surface away from drafts, flammable materials, and direct sunlight.

- **Storage:** Store candles in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight to prevent the wax from melting or discoloring.

- **Extinguishing:** Use a candle snuffer to extinguish the flame. Blowing out a candle can cause the wax to splatter and create smoke.
