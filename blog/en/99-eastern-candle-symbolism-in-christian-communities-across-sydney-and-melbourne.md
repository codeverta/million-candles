## **In-Depth Discussion**

### **1. Historical Background of Eastern Candles**

Eastern candles, commonly associated with Orthodox Christianity, originated from ancient practices in regions such as Greece, Armenia, Russia, Serbia, Georgia, and the Middle East. As migrants from these regions settled in Australia—especially in Sydney’s Inner West and Melbourne’s northern suburbs—they brought their religious customs with them.

#### **1.1 The Role of Light in Eastern Christian Theology**

Within Eastern Christian doctrine, the candle flame symbolizes:

* **Christ as the Light of the World**
* **Divine truth illuminating human life**
* **Spiritual purification and renewal**
* **Victory of light over darkness**

This belief remains central in Orthodox parishes in suburbs like Marrickville (Sydney) and Brunswick (Melbourne), where candle rituals form a core part of worship.

### **3. Key Eastern Candle Symbols Explained**

#### **3.1 Trikirion (Three-Branched Candle)**

Symbolizes the **Holy Trinity**.
Used during:

* Easter services
* Major blessings
* Liturgical processions

It is often seen in Greek Orthodox communities in Sydney’s Kingsford and Melbourne’s Oakleigh.

#### **3.2 Dikirion (Two-Branched Candle)**

Represents the **dual nature of Christ**—fully divine and fully human.
Bishops use this candle during:

* Gospel readings
* Blessings
* Special feasts

#### **3.3 Paschal Candle**

Although more associated with Western Christianity, many Eastern communities in Australia incorporate Paschal candles with symbols such as:

* Alpha (Α) and Omega (Ω)
* A cross
* Five wax nails

The Paschal candle symbolizes:

* Resurrection
* Christ’s eternal life
* Hope and salvation

#### **3.4 Beeswax Candles**

A large portion of Eastern Christian parishes in Melbourne and Sydney prefer **100 percent beeswax candles** due to their:

* Natural purity
* Clean burn
* Symbolic connection to Christ’s sinless nature

Beeswax candles are often handmade or imported from Greece, Lebanon, and Ukraine.

### **4. Eastern Candle Rituals in Australia**

#### **4.1 Pascha (Easter) Candle Ceremony**

One of the most breathtaking religious events in Australia is the Easter night celebration. At midnight, churches fall into complete darkness before the priest lights a single candle from the altar. The flame is passed throughout the congregation, illuminating the church with hundreds of glowing tapers.

This symbolizes:

* Christ’s resurrection
* Triumph over death
* Renewal of life and hope

In Sydney’s Greek Orthodox Parish of “The Resurrection of Christ” in Kogarah and Melbourne’s Holy Trinity Russian Orthodox Cathedral, this ritual draws thousands of worshippers each year.

#### **4.2 Baptism Candle Tradition**

During baptisms, a single candle is lit to symbolize:

* The child receiving divine light
* A spiritual journey filled with guidance and protection

Families in Australia often decorate baptism candles with lace, icons, flowers, or satin ribbons.

#### **4.3 Memorial Candles**

Eastern Christian families in Australia commonly light memorial candles at home or in church to:

* Remember their departed loved ones
* Pray for peace and mercy
* Honour ancestral heritage brought from overseas

Sydney’s Orthodox community in Liverpool and Melbourne’s Footscray have strong traditions of lighting memorial candles during major feast days.
